# Measuring squat depth with ordinary phone cameras

**CBSE Science Exhibition 2026–27 · AI for Better Life**

How do camera count and movement speed affect the accuracy of vertical squat-depth
estimates? We use ordinary phones, a measured checkerboard and a torch. After
calibration and synchronization, RTMPose locates each hip in each picture.
Triangulation estimates the hip midpoint in three dimensions. The vertical
drop from standing to the bottom gives one depth per repetition.

The planned pilot compares each camera and camera pair using the same timed
attempts. A visible pelvis marker and a measured vertical scale provide a
separate reading. A stool sets the task height. The reader records standing
and bottom positions and uncertainty before seeing the reconstruction.
We compare displacement in centimetres and separately score a fixed 3 cm
tolerance against the identified baseline repetition. Reports count missed,
unclear and unscorable attempts.

**Evidence to date.** Synthetic geometry checks verify software behavior with
known inputs. The simulation predicts outcomes under stated assumptions.
The pilot has no verified real-phone accuracy result. The student will record
the actual stick errors, marker readings, descent times and attempt counts
when the pilot takes place. The website analysis example is sample display data.

The simulation's “first detectable grid value” is the first tested shallower
depth where a threshold chosen from one half of simulated sessions flags at
least 95% of all shallow attempts and at most 5% of all matching attempts. The
other half checks that fixed threshold. Unclear attempts remain in the
denominators and count as not detected. Three test squats in one session share
their reference squat, so uncertainty intervals resample whole sessions. This
is a discrete grid result, not interpolation or a claim about real cameras; a
separate fixed 3 cm software tolerance is also reported.
